Abstract

Anti-money laundering (AML) compliance increasingly relies on adverse media screening — the process of identifying negative news associated with entities to flag potential financial crime risk. Large language models are being evaluated and deployed for this task, but their hallucination behavior in this high-stakes context is poorly understood.

This paper presents a pre-registered evaluation of hallucination rates across three frontier models — Claude Sonnet 4.6, Claude Opus 4.7, and GPT-4o-mini — using 100 synthetic entities spanning 5 ground-truth categories. We find a 0% false positive rate on clean entities, but identify two novel hallucination failure modes: PEP inference hallucination (50% rate on Claude Sonnet) and template-regurgitation hallucination (67% on GPT-4o-mini). Claude Opus 4.7 showed 0% hallucination across all categories.

Study Design

100 synthetic entities were constructed across 5 ground-truth categories, designed to probe specific hallucination failure modes without using real individuals or organisations. Each model was asked to perform an adverse media screening assessment for each entity, and outputs were evaluated against ground-truth labels by a blinded reviewer.

Key Findings

0% False Positive Rate on Clean Entities

All three models correctly avoided flagging clean entities as adverse, suggesting that the basic false-positive problem (flagging innocent parties) is well-controlled. The more dangerous failure modes are subtler.

PEP Inference Hallucination — 50% Rate on Claude Sonnet

Claude Sonnet 4.6 hallucinated politically exposed person (PEP) status for entities that were politically adjacent but not themselves PEPs, at a rate of 50% on the PEP-adjacent category. This represents a novel failure mode where the model infers risk from contextual association rather than direct evidence.

Template-Regurgitation Hallucination — 67% Rate on GPT-4o-mini

GPT-4o-mini produced fabricated but structurally plausible adverse media summaries for 67% of ambiguous entities — generating content that matched the expected format of an adverse media report without grounding in actual evidence. This is a distinct failure mode from factual confabulation.

Claude Opus 4.7 — 0% Hallucination Across All Categories

Claude Opus 4.7 showed 0% hallucination across all five ground-truth categories, demonstrating that the failure modes identified are model-specific and not inherent to the task structure.

Primary Result
Hallucination in LLM-based AML screening is not uniformly distributed: models with strong overall performance may still exhibit category-specific failure modes (PEP inference, template regurgitation) that pose specific compliance risks invisible to aggregate accuracy metrics.

Implications for AML Practice

These findings have direct implications for compliance teams evaluating LLMs for adverse media screening:

  • Aggregate accuracy is insufficient. A model with 0% false positives on clean entities may still hallucinate in PEP-adjacent or ambiguous contexts.
  • Category-specific testing is required. Benchmark design must include the specific entity types most likely to trigger failure modes.
  • Model selection matters. The gap between Claude Opus 4.7 (0% hallucination) and smaller models (50–67% on specific categories) is significant for high-stakes deployments.
