Abstract

Frontier large language models are trained with safety guardrails that can cause them to refuse certain requests. A key question for alignment research is whether these refusal mechanisms are applied symmetrically across political and ideological frames, or whether models exhibit systematic bias in which types of requests they decline.

This paper presents a pre-registered evaluation measuring refusal rates across 20 matched request pairs — each pair containing a LEFT-coded and a RIGHT-coded framing of an economically equivalent policy question — across 3 frontier models (540 total API calls, blinded LLM-as-judge scoring).

We find a RIGHT-framing refusal rate of ~20% vs. 0% for LEFT-framing (Refusal Rate Differential RRD ≈ −0.16), consistent across Claude Opus 4.7, Claude Sonnet 4.6, and GPT-4o-mini, concentrated in tax, energy, housing, and fiscal policy topics.

Study Design

20 request pairs were constructed, each covering an economically equivalent policy question framed in two ways: one using language and framings conventionally associated with left-leaning economic positions, and one using language conventionally associated with right-leaning positions. The underlying factual content and request type were held constant across the pair.

All 20 pairs were submitted to each of 3 models (Claude Opus 4.7, Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-4o-mini), yielding 40 responses per model and 120 responses total per scoring pass. A blinded LLM judge scored each response for refusal vs. compliance, with no access to the model identity or framing condition.

Key Findings

Consistent RIGHT-Framing Refusal Bias Across All 3 Models

The asymmetry (RIGHT-coded requests refused more often than LEFT-coded equivalents) held across Claude Opus 4.7, Claude Sonnet 4.6, and GPT-4o-mini — suggesting a systematic pattern in frontier model training rather than a model-specific artifact.

0% Refusal Rate for LEFT-Framed Requests

No model refused any LEFT-coded request across any topic. The refusal asymmetry is entirely one-directional: right-coded requests are selectively refused while economically equivalent left-coded requests are uniformly complied with.

Concentrated in Four Topic Areas

The refusal asymmetry was concentrated in tax policy, energy, housing, and fiscal policy — topics where right-coded framings frequently involve deregulation, lower taxes, or market-based solutions. Topics such as trade and monetary policy showed lower or no asymmetry.

RRD ≈ −0.16 Consistent Across Models

The Refusal Rate Differential was approximately −0.16 when averaged across all models and topics, indicating that RIGHT-framed requests face roughly a 16 percentage point higher refusal probability than LEFT-framed equivalents on the same underlying policy question.

Key Implication
The refusal asymmetry identified here represents a form of politically asymmetric content moderation — not necessarily intentional, but emergent from training data and RLHF processes — with potential implications for how LLMs serve users across the political spectrum.
